About

I'm a high school senior doing independent research at the intersection of large language models and formal mathematics. I'm interested in autoformalization — the automated translation of natural-language mathematical statements into formal languages like Lean — and in understanding where and why current models are fragile, as a step toward building more reliable systems for machine-checked mathematics.

Research

Lean Paraphrases: Analyzing the Effect of Wording on Autoformalization Output

Leo Zeng

Recent research in autoformalization has placed less emphasis on the fact that Large Language Models are sensitive to slight changes in the wording of problems. Our work complements current research in this area by providing insight into the effects of paraphrasing problems on statement autoformalization. We first manually create a dataset of 50 paraphrased problem pairs, and manually check the outputs of model autoformalizations for consistency with the original problem statement. We then extend this study with an automated pipeline that generates and verifies 1000 additional paraphrase pairs drawn from the FormalMATH benchmark, replacing manual correctness checking with a dual-LLM judge. Across both the manual and automated studies, we find that slight rewordings have a significant impact on the structure of the resulting outputs, even if those outputs are accurate and consistent with the natural language problem statement, and that this sensitivity persists at scale. We argue for the need for a system or intermediary language in order to standardize the format of natural language problem statements, resulting in more reliable model autoformalizations.
